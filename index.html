About me

I am a final year PhD student at the Department of Computer Sciences, UW-Madison since 2021. I am very fortunate to be advised by Prof. Christos Tzamos and Prof. Ilias Diakonikolas. I obtained my B.S. in Mathematics in 2020 from the Department of Mathematics at Nanjing University. Before officially starting my Ph.D. study, I spent a year as a visiting student at ITCS, Shanghai University of Finance and Economics.

Research Interests

My research focuses on the algorithmic and theoretical foundations of machine learning and artificial intelligence. I use tools from theoretical computer science, statistics, and optimization to design efficient and reliable learning algorithms or characterize hardness for modern machine learning problems. My current research interests are:

Robust Learning and Information-Computation Tradeoff
Many learning problems can be solved with good sample complexity. However, designing learning algorithms that achieve both sample efficiency and computational efficiency can be much more challenging and sometimes impossible. Such a phenomenon frequently happens when label noise or distribution uncertainty is introduced. I am interested in understanding when designing efficient and robust learning algorithms is possible and when information-computation tradeoff appears.

Foundations and Applications of Interactive Learning
Interactions play a fundamental role in many machine learning problems and are usually used to speed up learning process when data is redundant. I am interested in understanding what kind of interactions are sufficient and necessary to achieve such improvements. I am also interested in using the ideas of interactive learning to design better learning algorithm for problems, where interactions are usually not considered.

Principle Understandings of LLMs
Although modern language models have shown amazing capacities, we lack of a good understanding of them in many ways. Together with my collaborators, we recently focus on hybrid architectures in LLMs. Using theoretical and empirical tools, we study when hybrid models are better choices than classic pure models and how layer ordering designs affect their performance.
